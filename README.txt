Lightkeeper does four things to lights you have already paired. It turns a remote, switch or dial you already own into a controller for them: pick the lights, and say what each press, hold or turn should do. It puts lights on a schedule: when they come on, when they go off, on the days you choose, at a brightness and warmth you set. It follows the colour of the day, either from three parts of the day anchored to your own sunrise and sunset, or from a curve you draw yourself. And it sets brightness from how much light is in the room already, reading a light sensor you own — it shows you that sensor's own last week while you choose — or how high the sun is where you live.

Light Remotes and schedules write the Homey Flows for you and keep them maintained, so you never open the Flow editor. Homey does not let an app create Flows on its own, so those two need a Personal API Key with Flow permissions; circadian, Colour Curve and Room-sensing Lights write to your lights directly and need no key. Setting any of the five up is a short numbered path — one question a screen, then a review of everything before it saves. Everything runs on your Homey — no cloud, no telemetry. Designed and written with AI, directed and verified on real hardware.
