Lightkeeper does four things to lights you have already paired. It turns a remote, switch or dial you already own into a controller for them: pick the lights, and say what each press, hold or turn should do. It puts lights on a schedule: when they come on, when they go off, on the days you choose, at a brightness and warmth you set. It follows the colour of the day, either from two settings — how warm, how cool — or from a curve you draw yourself. And it sets brightness from how much light is in the room already, reading any light sensors you own, or how high the sun is where you live.

Light controllers and schedules write the Homey Flows for you and keep them maintained, so you never open the Flow editor. Homey does not let an app create Flows on its own, so those two need a Personal API Key with Flow permissions; circadian, Curve and Daylight lights write to your lights directly and need no key. A schedule, a circadian light or a Curve light can take its brightness from the daylight too, on whichever windows or points you choose. Everything runs on your Homey — no cloud, no telemetry. Designed and written with AI, directed and verified on real hardware.
