This app exists because I bought three scroll wheel remotes from IKEA. Three remotes, three lights each, three things I wanted each remote to do — which, once I opened the Flow editor, added up to 27 Flows to build by hand. Half an hour of clicking, maybe an hour. I couldn't be bothered. So I spent a couple of days building an app instead. Or rather, I had AI build one.

It does three jobs, across four kinds of device. The first: pick a remote, switch or dial you have already paired, pick the lights you want it to control, and say what each press, hold or turn should do — on, off, brighter, dimmer, warmer, cooler. The second: pick lights and put them on a timer. Say when they come on, and whether they go off after a while or at a time you choose, on the days you choose, optionally at a brightness and warmth you set. Up to twelve windows on one schedule, and a switch on the device to pause the lot while you are away.

The third job is the colour of the day, and it comes in two forms. A circadian light asks two questions — what your lights should look like at their warmest and at their coolest — and handles the rest of the day itself: warm overnight, cooling through the morning, cool through the middle, warming again from mid-afternoon. A Curve light is the same thing with the whole day open to you: set what your lights should look like at every point you choose, and give any of those points a colour instead of a warmth — candle, amber, peach, rose, lavender, ocean, forest, ember. Lights that cannot show a colour follow the warmth instead, so the day looks the same shape on every lamp. Either way your lights are the right colour the moment you switch them on and keep adjusting while they are on, neither one ever switches a light on or off, and neither needs an API key.

Every kind of device has a Test button that drives the real lights immediately, so you know it works before you save. For the first two, Lightkeeper writes the Flows underneath and keeps them maintained as things change — and if you move a lamp into a room a device points at, it follows. You never open the Flow editor.

What it needs: a Homey Pro 2023 or later, and — for the remotes and the schedules — a Personal API Key with Flow permissions. That second one is unusual, so it is worth saying why: Homey does not let an app create Flows on its own, and generating Flows is the trick behind those two. A circadian light and a Curve light create none, so neither needs a key at all. The key stays on your Homey, is used only for writing Flows, and is never logged or included in a diagnostics report. If it ever stops working your remotes keep controlling your lights and your schedules keep firing; only Flow maintenance pauses until you paste in a new one, and you lose nothing you set up.

No telemetry, no cloud, nothing leaves your Homey. Designed and written with AI, directed and verified on real hardware by me. The full source is public, so you can check all of that rather than take my word for it.
